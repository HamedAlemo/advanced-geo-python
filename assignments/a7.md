## Background: fields, and a tale of two regimes

Fields of The World (FTW) is a global, 10 m map of agricultural field boundaries produced by running a deep learning model (a U-Net) on Sentinel-2 imagery. It is not a cadastral or legal layer. A field here is a remote-sensing unit, so one real parcel may map to many polygons or to none, and every field comes with a model confidence score.

That makes the France versus Kenya comparison the heart of this assignment. France is dominated by large, mechanized, geometrically regular fields, the kind of landscape the model sees often and predicts with high confidence. Kenya is dominated by smallholder agriculture with small, irregular plots, a regime where the FTW authors themselves note that confidence is lower and more conservative. So when you compare the two countries you are reading two things at once: a real difference in how the two landscapes are farmed, and a difference in how certain an ML model is about what it is seeing. Keeping those two apart is the analytical point.

## The data

FTW Global is hosted on Source Cooperative as GeoParquet, partitioned one file per country. The data is anonymous read over HTTPS, no account needed.

Vector base path:
```
https://data.source.coop/ftw/global-data/predictions/vectors/alpha/results-by-admin-conf/
```
The two country files for this assignment:
```
.../results-by-admin-conf/admin:country_code=FR/France.parquet
.../results-by-admin-conf/admin:country_code=KE/Kenya.parquet
```

```{note}
The folder name contains a colon (`admin:country_code=FR`). If your HTTP client objects to it, URL-encode the colon as `%3A`. Confirm the exact file names and paths against the dataset's own `README` and `llms.txt` at https://data.source.coop/ftw/global-data/ before you hard-code them, since cloud datasets do get reorganized.
```

Columns include: `id`, `geometry`, `bbox`, `metrics:area`, `metrics:perimeter`, `determination:datetime`, `determination:method`, `admin:country_code`, `admin:subdivision_code`, and `confidence`.

Two things to internalize before you compute anything. First, the geometry CRS is `EPSG:4326`, so coordinates are in degrees, but `metrics:area` is provided in square meters. You will use that mismatch in Part 1. Second, `determination:datetime` carries the prediction year (2024 and 2025 exist); pick a single year and use it for both countries so the comparison is fair.
